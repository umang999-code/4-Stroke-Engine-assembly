# Working Principle of 4-Stroke IC Engine:

# Overview

A 4-Stroke Internal Combustion (IC) Engine completes one complete operating cycle in four strokes of the piston. 
The piston moves between Top Dead Centre (TDC) and Bottom Dead Centre (BDC).
The four strokes are:

1. Intake (Suction) Stroke
2. Compression Stroke
3. Power (Expansion) Stroke
4. Exhaust Stroke
A complete cycle requires two revolutions (720°) of the crankshaft.


1. # Intake (Suction) Stroke
During the intake stroke, the piston moves from TDC → BDC.
Inlet valve: Open
Exhaust valve: Closed
Fresh air-fuel mixture enters the cylinder in an SI (petrol) engine.
The cylinder is filled with the fresh charge.
# Key Point: The piston moves downward and draws the fresh charge into the cylinder.


2. # Compression Stroke
During the compression stroke, the piston moves from BDC → TDC.
Inlet valve: Closed
Exhaust valve: Closed
The fresh charge is compressed inside the cylinder.
Compression increases the pressure and temperature of the charge.
# Key Point: Both valves remain closed and the piston compresses the charge.

3. # Power(Expansion)Stroke
The compressed charge is ignited near the end of the compression stroke.
Inlet valve: Closed
Exhaust valve: Closed
Combustion produces high-pressure gases.
The gases push the piston from TDC → BDC.
The connecting rod transfers this force to the crankshaft.
The crankshaft converts the piston’s reciprocating motion into rotary motion.

# Key Point: This is the power-producing stroke of the engine.

4. # Exhaust Stroke
During the exhaust stroke, the piston moves from BDC → TDC.
Inlet valve: Closed
Exhaust valve: Open
Burnt gases are pushed out of the cylinder through the exhaust valve.
# Key Point: The piston moves upward and removes the burnt gases from the cylinder.
